## Independently checked tractable routes

### Unique nearest point implies pointwise distance differentiability

This route is mathematically checked but not yet a Lean proof. Let A be nonempty compact, let y be outside A, and suppose x is its unique nearest point. At each nearby u, choose any nearest point a(u) using compactness. Every cluster point of a(u) as u tends to y minimises distance from y, by continuity of distance and of the infimum-distance function. Uniqueness at y therefore forces a(u) to tend to x. No uniqueness at nearby u is asserted.

The two minimising inequalities give

`infDist(u,A)^2 − infDist(y,A)^2 − 2⟪y−x,u−y⟫ ≤ ‖u−y‖²`

and

`infDist(u,A)^2 − infDist(y,A)^2 − 2⟪y−x,u−y⟫ ≥ −2‖u−y‖‖a(u)−x‖`.

The first uses x as a competitor at u. The second expands around a(u), uses minimality of x at y, and bounds the remaining inner product; dropping a nonnegative squared term is legitimate. Thus the absolute remainder divided by ‖u−y‖ tends to zero. The derivative of squared distance is the functional `v ↦ 2⟪y−x,v⟫`. Since the actual distance at y is positive, composition with square root gives the radial distance gradient in the manuscript. The final implementation must keep this as an actual infimum-distance theorem, rather than define distance to be distance from a chosen point.

The differentiability obligation is tractable with the checked compactness and remainder APIs even though no single library theorem was found. Absence of that API is not evidence that the source assertion is false.
