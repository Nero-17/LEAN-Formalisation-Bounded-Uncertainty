## Independently checked proposed one-dimensional counterexample

The parent's proposal X=[-1,1], f=id, ε(x)=(1-x²)/2 satisfies every system assumption except d>=2. The set is regular closed, the polynomial maps have smooth local extensions, ε ranges in [0,1/2], and ε(x)<=1-|x| ensures every radius interval stays in X. Its derivative is -x and has magnitude one at the endpoints. On the two unit directions the update is respectively x+ε(x) and x-ε(x), with the normal unchanged. Both maps fix the endpoints. For x<y in [-1,1], their differences are (y-x)(1-(x+y)/2) and (y-x)(1+(x+y)/2), both strictly positive since -2<x+y<2. Continuous strict monotonicity and the endpoints give bijections of the interval. This validates the mathematical target; it does not count as a Lean proof until the actual implementation is checked.
